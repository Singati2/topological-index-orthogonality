\begin{abstract}
On molecular graphs no atom has more than four bonds, so an edge has one
of ten degree types, and every bond-incident-degree (BID) index
$\sum_{uv\in E} f(d_u,d_v)$ --- Zagreb, Randi\'c, Sombor and their
hundreds of variants --- is a linear combination of the ten edge-type
counts $m_{ij}$ (Gutman 2013; Rada 2026). We turn this known fact into a
checkable \emph{span certificate}: a rank condition on a baseline of
classical indices which, when it holds, makes every BID index, for every
$f$ and every parameter value, an exact linear combination of the
baseline, with zero residual, infinite variance inflation and no
residual direction that could correlate with any target. A standard
$18$-index degree-based baseline satisfies the certificate for all graphs
of maximum degree four, hence on all molecular datasets without
hypervalent atoms; we verify it on \nGenDatasets{} MoleculeNet SMILES
datasets (\nGenMolecules{} molecules, including QM9 and MUV), where it
holds on \nGenCertified{} and fails exactly on the one (HIV) containing
\hivHyper{} organometallic molecules with atoms of degree up to ten. A
census of $14$ published degree-based indices ($15$ instances, eight
Sombor-type) and a three-parameter family confirms exact redundancy to
relative residuals below $2\times10^{-12}$, exposes a numerical pitfall
that turns exact zeros into spurious partial correlations of order
$10^{-2}$, and gives each index its coefficient vector; a one-function
implementation is provided. For indices outside the BID class the
question is empirical: of $20$ alternative candidates, $15$--$17$ per
dataset pass a pairwise screen; against the topological baseline two
show residual association under both linear and cross-fitted non-linear
residualization, but a null distribution of exactly redundant draws
reveals a leakage floor of $0.08$--$0.13$ for the non-linear statistic,
against which neither is significant after multiplicity correction; and
none passes once standard RDKit 2D descriptors enter the baseline. Finally, hydrogen-suppressed unlabelled
graphs collide: $56\%$ (ESOL) and $74\%$ (FreeSolv) of molecules share
their graph with another, and the in-sample graph oracle bounds the
variance any topology-only model can explain at $R^2\le\oracleRsqEsol$
and $\oracleRsqFsv$, below what a chemistry-aware random forest reaches
out of sample ($\rdkitRsqEsol$, $\rdkitRsqFsv$).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} Topological index; degree-based index;
Sombor index; redundancy; partial correlation; QSPR; molecular
descriptors.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
% =====================================================================

Scalar topological indices have grown from a handful of early
proposals~\cite{Wiener1947,Gutman1972,Randic1975} to several hundred,
and the proliferation has been criticised within mathematical
chemistry itself~\cite{Gutman2013Degree}. The growth is fastest for
degree-based indices: since the Sombor index was introduced in
2021~\cite{Gutman2021Sombor}, modified, $p$-, KG-, elliptic, Euler,
diminished and hyperbolic Sombor variants have followed
~\cite{KulliGutman2021Modified,GutmanFurtulaOz2024Elliptic,Gutman2024Euler,MovahediGutmanRedzepovicFurtula2026,BarmanDas2026Hyperbolic,RetiDoslicAli2021,KulliHarishChaluvarajuGutman2022KG}. A new index is commonly supported by a correlation with a
property on a small isomer set, by bounds and extremal graphs, and
by its correlations with existing indices, e.g.\ intercorrelation
tables among Sombor-type variants~\cite{Redzepovic2021ChemSombor,MovahediGutmanRedzepovicFurtula2026}. Quality criteria that
go beyond single correlations exist --- structure
sensitivity and abruptness~\cite{FurtulaGutmanDehmer2013}, applicability
tests on octanes~\cite{KumarDas2024Structure}, and clustering-based
usefulness scores~\cite{DehmerEmmertStreibTripathi2013,Ma2022Usefulness} --- but
none of them answers the question a QSPR modeller needs answered:
\emph{does the new index carry information about a target that an
explicit baseline does not already carry?}

For the largest class of indices this question turns out to be
settled before any data are examined. It is classical that a
bond-incident-degree (BID) index
$I_f(G)=\sum_{uv\in E(G)} f(d_u,d_v)$ is the linear combination
$\sum_{i\le j} f(i,j)\,m_{ij}(G)$ of the edge-degree-pair counts
$m_{ij}$~\cite{Gutman2013Degree,RadaCruz2014VDB}, and the resulting
finite-dimensional linear view of vertex-degree-based indices has
recently been developed in general~\cite{CruzEspinalRada2024Matrix,Rada2026LinearVDB},
including the observation that the analytic form of $f$ is irrelevant
once its values on the degree pairs are fixed. If a baseline of
classical degree-based indices spans the edge-degree-pair counts
realised in a dataset --- a rank condition that does not involve the
candidate or the target --- then \emph{every} BID index, whatever its
functional form or parameters, is an exact linear combination of the
baseline on that dataset, and a target-aware redundancy screen has
nothing to measure. We call this rank condition a \emph{span
certificate}: a baseline is \emph{certified} on a dataset when the
condition holds, and a candidate is then \emph{certified redundant}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item \emph{Span certificate} (Section~\ref{sec:theory}). The
mathematics is not ours: the linearity of BID indices in the counts
$m_{ij}$ and the finite-dimensional framework built on it are due to
Gutman~\cite{Gutman2013Degree}, Rada and
Cruz~\cite{RadaCruz2014VDB} and Rada~\cite{Rada2026LinearVDB}, and that
framework contains no statistical or dataset-level analysis. What is
ours is the statistical reading: an explicit, checkable rank condition
on a baseline under which every BID index has zero residual, infinite
variance inflation and no residual direction towards any target
(Proposition~\ref{prop:cert}); its data-free form for graphs of
maximum degree at most four, with the explicit coefficient vectors
(Corollary~\ref{cor:datafree}, which we have not found stated in this
form); the observation that a standard $18$-index baseline satisfies
it; and its verification on benchmark data.
\item \emph{Generality} (Section~\ref{sec:general}). The certificate
is checked on \nGenDatasets{} MoleculeNet SMILES datasets
(\nGenMolecules{} molecules): it holds on \nGenCertified{}, and the one
failure (HIV) is caused by \hivHyper{} organometallic molecules with
atoms of degree five to ten --- the case the data-free certificate
excludes and the data-level check catches.
\item \emph{Census} (Section~\ref{sec:census}). Fourteen published
degree-based indices ($15$ instances, eight of them Sombor-type) and $100$ points of a
three-parameter family, on four MoleculeNet datasets: all exactly
redundant, which sharpens earlier correlational reports of near-redundancy
into an exact statement; two non-BID Sombor-type controls correctly
fall outside the span.
\item \emph{A numerical pitfall and a tool} (Section~\ref{sec:pitfall}).
For exactly redundant indices, partial correlations computed with
absolute residual tolerances are noise of order $10^{-2}$; we give a
numerically safe recipe and a one-function implementation of the
certificate that returns each index's coefficient vector.
\item \emph{Target-aware screen for non-BID indices}
(Section~\ref{sec:nonbid}). Confidence intervals against an
effect-size margin, a cross-fitted non-linear residualization with a
proper null distribution, and a repeat of the screen against a
chemistry-aware (RDKit 2D) baseline, on $20$ alternative candidates.
\item \emph{Information ceiling} (Section~\ref{sec:ceiling}). Graph
collisions under hydrogen suppression, confirmed by exact isomorphism,
give an in-sample oracle for the whole topology-only representation; a
graph-grouped, corrected benchmark places the indices against this
oracle and against an atom- and bond-aware descriptor reference.
\end{enumerate}

The paper is a companion to a mathematical study of the
three-parameter Loyola family~\cite{Paper1_Loyola}, which concludes
on independent grounds that its imbalance parameter is an
interpretable, analytically tractable direction rather than a
predictive upgrade; the span certificate explains why no BID family can add
linearly to a classical baseline. (The two papers share authors; the
companion paper is in preparation.)

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{Redundancy and orthogonalization.} Strong mutual
correlation among topological indices is long established. Hollas
studied the redundancy of graph invariants used in
chemoinformatics~\cite{Hollas2006Redundancy} and related the
covariance of degree-based indices to the edge structure of the
graph~\cite{Hollas2005Covariance}; orthogonalization of descriptors is
an equally old remedy~\cite{Lucic1997Orthogonal,Du2002Orthogonal}.
Recent work continues to report intercorrelations among new
variants, e.g.\ among Sombor-type indices on octanes~\cite{MovahediGutmanRedzepovicFurtula2026,Redzepovic2021ChemSombor},
and large-scale descriptor evaluations cluster descriptors without
reference to a target~\cite{DehmerEmmertStreibTripathi2013,Ma2022Usefulness}.
We do not claim to discover that topological indices are redundant.

\paragraph{Linear structure of degree-based indices.} The identity
$I_f=\sum f(i,j)m_{ij}$ is standard~\cite{Gutman2013Degree,RadaCruz2014VDB}
and underlies the bond-additive modelling programme in which the
term ``bond incident degree'' index originates~\cite{VukicevicGasperov2010Adriatic,VukicevicDjurdjevic2011BID}.
Matrix and finite-dimensional linear formulations of
vertex-degree-based indices, including their discrimination
properties, are developed in~\cite{CruzEspinalRada2024Matrix,Rada2026LinearVDB};
the M-polynomial~\cite{DeutschKlavzar2015} is the generating
function of the same counts; further algebraic constructions on
degree-based indices appear in~\cite{GutmanBuragohainNyauli2026Concealed};
and the edge-type-count view underlies recent extremal-graph work for
chemical graphs of maximum degree three~\cite{Bonte2025Extremal,Dusollier2025Polyhedral,Bonte2025ChemicHull}. The boundary between that literature and the present paper is stated
once, in Contribution~(1).

\paragraph{Degeneracy.} That distinct graphs can share index values
(degeneracy) and that hydrogen suppression merges distinct molecules
are classical concerns~\cite{Konstantinova1996,Pogliani2008,Dehmer2012Degeneracy};
a recent audit of the MoleculeNet benchmarks documents duplicate and
conflicting entries at the SMILES level~\cite{SchuhDanilukSieber2026}.
Section~\ref{sec:ceiling} quantifies collisions at the level of the
hydrogen-suppressed graph (isomorphism classes), which is the relevant
level for topological indices, and uses them as a ceiling for the whole
topology-only representation rather than for a single index.

\paragraph{Descriptor evaluation in QSPR.} Topological descriptors
remain competitive baselines in modern property
prediction~\cite{AdamczykCzech2024MOLTOP,PraskiAdamczykCzech2025}, which keeps the question of
which indices are worth adding practically relevant. Generic feature
selection (LASSO~\cite{Tibshirani1996}, mRMR~\cite{PengLongDing2005},
Boruta~\cite{KursaRudnicki2010}) acts on a fixed descriptor matrix;
the screen here acts one layer earlier, on whether a proposed index
should enter that matrix at all.

\section{Data, curation and baseline}\label{sec:data}

We use four MoleculeNet benchmarks~\cite{MoleculeNet}: ESOL (aqueous
solubility)~\cite{Delaney2004ESOL}, FreeSolv (experimental hydration

formed, and molecules with fewer than two heavy atoms are dropped, so

atom identity are invisible to every descriptor studied here, so
molecules that differ only in these respects become duplicates; we
report all screening results on the files as distributed and verify
in Section~\ref{sec:nonbid} that de-duplication on canonical SMILES
(averaging regression duplicates whose labels agree within $0.5$ target
units, dropping duplicate groups that disagree by more or carry
conflicting BBBP labels) changes no conclusion; the benchmark of
Section~\ref{sec:ceiling} uses the curated data. All molecules have maximum degree at most four
after hydrogen suppression.

\section{The span certificate}\label{sec:theory}
% =====================================================================

Let $\Gc_\Delta$ be the class of finite simple graphs without
isolated vertices and with maximum degree at most $\Delta$, and
$P_\Delta=\{(i,j):1\le i\le j\le\Delta\}$, so $|P_4|=10$. For
$G\in\Gc_\Delta$ let $m_{ij}(G)$ be the number of edges whose end
degrees are $\{i,j\}$. A \emph{BID index} is
$I_f(G)=\sum_{uv\in E(G)} f(d_u,d_v)$ for a symmetric
$f:\{1,\dots,\Delta\}^2\to\mathbb{R}$.

\begin{remark}[classical]\label{rem:linear}
$I_f(G)=\sum_{(i,j)\in P_\Delta} f(i,j)\,m_{ij}(G)$, because the
edge classes $\{uv:\{d_u,d_v\}=\{i,j\}\}$ partition $E(G)$ and $f$ is
constant on each~\cite{Gutman2013Degree,RadaCruz2014VDB}. For example, the path on four vertices has two edges of type $(1,2)$
and one of type $(2,2)$, so $I_f=2f(1,2)+f(2,2)$ on it for every $f$, and the star
$K_{1,3}$ has three edges of type $(1,3)$, so $I_f(K_{1,3})=3f(1,3)$.
On $\Gc_4$ the ten functionals $m_{ij}$ are linearly independent: the complete
bipartite graph $K_{i,j}$ has all $ij$ edges of type $(i,j)$, so its
count vector is a multiple of the $(i,j)$-th unit vector. Hence the BID
indices on $\Gc_4$ form exactly a ten-dimensional space; on connected
graphs with at least three vertices $m_{11}=0$ and the space is at
most nine-dimensional.
\end{remark}

Now fix a dataset $D=\{G_1,\dots,G_n\}\subset\Gc_\Delta$ with
realised pair set $P_D\subseteq P_\Delta$, count matrix
$M\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times|P_D|}$, and a baseline whose BID block
consists of $k$ indices with value matrix
$\Phi_B\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times|P_D|}$, $(\Phi_B)_{b,(i,j)}=f_b(i,j)$. The
baseline's BID columns are $B=M\Phi_B^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}$.
Write $\mathbf 1$ for the all-ones vector.

The following is an elementary consequence of Remark~\ref{rem:linear};
its statistical reading (i)--(iv) is the point.

\begin{proposition}[Span certificate]\label{prop:cert}
If $\operatorname{rank}[\mathbf 1, B]=\operatorname{rank}[\mathbf 1,M]$,
then for every symmetric $f$ the vector $z_f=(I_f(G_t))_{t\le n}$
lies in $\col[\mathbf 1,B]$. Consequently, for any baseline matrix
$X$ whose columns include $B$ and for every target vector $y$:
(i) the least-squares residual of $z_f$ on $[\mathbf 1,X]$ is zero;
(ii) if $z_f$ is not constant on $D$, its variance inflation relative to
$X$ is infinite;
(iii) the partial correlation $\pcor(z_f,y\mid X)$ --- the correlation
between the residuals of $z_f$ and of $y$ after least-squares regression
on $[\mathbf 1,X]$ --- is undefined ($0/0$):
$z_f$ carries no direction beyond $X$, and we report it as $0$ by
convention --- any nonzero value computed in floating point is an
artefact (Section~\ref{sec:pitfall}); and
(iv) adding $z_f$ to $X$ leaves every ordinary least-squares fit
(fitted values, residuals, $R^2$) on $D$ unchanged. Penalised linear
fits (ridge, LASSO) can change, but only through the geometry of the
penalty, not through any new direction in the data.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Remark~\ref{rem:linear}, $z_f=Mf_D$ with $f_D=(f(i,j))_{(i,j)\in P_D}$,
so $z_f\in\col(M)$. Since $B=M\Phi_B^{\top}$, $\col[\mathbf 1,B]\subseteq\col[\mathbf 1,M]$,
and equal ranks give equality. Thus $z_f\in\col[\mathbf 1,B]\subseteq\col[\mathbf 1,X]$,
which is (i); (ii) and (iii) restate (i), and (iv) holds because an
OLS fit depends on the design only through its column space.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[data-free certificate on $\Gc_4$]\label{cor:datafree}
If $\operatorname{rank}\Phi_B=10$ over $P_4$, then for every symmetric
$f$ there is a coefficient vector $c$, unique up to $\ker \Phi_B^{\top}$, with
$\Phi_B^{\top}c=f_{P_4}$, and $I_f(G)=\sum_b c_b\,I_{f_b}(G)$ holds
\emph{identically} on $\Gc_4$. In particular the conclusions of
Proposition~\ref{prop:cert} hold on every dataset of graphs in
$\Gc_4$, without inspecting the data.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$\operatorname{rank}\Phi_B=10$ means the rows $f_b$ span
$\mathbb{R}^{P_4}$; then
\[
I_f=\sum_{ij}f(i,j)\,m_{ij}=\sum_{ij}\sum_b c_b\, f_b(i,j)\, m_{ij}=\sum_b c_b\, I_{f_b}.
\]
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The baseline satisfies the certificate.} Evaluating each of
the $18$ degree-based baseline indices on $K_{i,j}$ gives $\Phi_B$
exactly (Supplementary Table~S2); its rank over $P_4$ is $10$ (smallest-to-largest singular
value ratio $1.2\times10^{-4}$), so Corollary~\ref{cor:datafree}
applies. The certificate is generic rather than delicate: $37\,322$ of
the $43\,758$ ten-element subsets of the $18$ indices already have rank
$10$, for example $\{M_1,M_2,mM_1,mM_2,F,R,\mathrm{SCI},H,GA,AG\}$. On
the data, the numerical ranks of $M$, $B$ and $[B,M]$ coincide on all
four datasets (Table~\ref{tab:cert}).

\begin{remark}[what the certificate does and does not say]\label{rem:scope}
The certificate is a statement about \emph{linear} redundancy. It
does not say that a BID index is useless: a parsimonious index can
summarise the ten counts well, and a model that is not
rotation-invariant (e.g.\ a tree ensemble) may use a BID index
differently from its constituents. It does say that no BID index can
add linearly to a certified baseline, so linear redundancy
diagnostics --- pairwise correlation, the variance inflation factor
(VIF), partial correlation, and
the gain of an ordinary least-squares model --- are decided in advance
for this class.
The certificate covers edge-additive indices only; it does not
cover normalised or averaged indices such as $I_f/|E|$, multiplicative
indices $\prod f$, non-linear transforms such as $\log I_f$, products
of indices, neighbourhood-degree-sum and other second-order indices,
or valence- and heteroatom-weighted degrees. Beyond $\Delta=4$
(hypervalent atoms, explicit hydrogens, coordination compounds) the space has dimension $\Delta(\Delta+1)/2$
and the certificate must be checked on the data, which
Proposition~\ref{prop:cert} makes a one-line rank computation.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Scope check across MoleculeNet}\label{sec:general}

The data-free certificate covers every dataset of graphs with maximum
degree four. To see how far that reaches, and what happens beyond it,
we ran the data-level check of Proposition~\ref{prop:cert} on every
SMILES-based MoleculeNet dataset we could obtain (Table~\ref{tab:general};
only SMILES are used, no targets; \nGenMolecules{} molecules in all).
For the eight datasets whose molecules all have maximum degree four
--- including the $133\,882$ molecules of QM9 and the $93\,087$ of MUV ---
Corollary~\ref{cor:datafree} guarantees the outcome, and the run is a
numerical check of it: the ranks of $M$ and $B$ agree and every
$m_{ij}$ column has relative residual at most $\genMaxResid$. The
informative cases are the other five. Four datasets (ClinTox, SIDER,
Tox21, ToxCast) contain one to three molecules with an atom of degree
five or six; here certification is data-dependent, because as functions
on $P_6$ the $18$ baseline functions have rank only $17$
($\sigma=F-2M_2$ exactly), so at most seven pairs beyond $P_4$ can ever
be absorbed. These datasets realise one to three such pairs, the
baseline's rank rises by the same amount, and the certificate holds. The single
failure is HIV: \hivHyper{} of its $41\,127$ molecules are organometallic
complexes (Co, Ni, Cu, Rh, Fe, Mn, Cr, Mo, Ir centres with five to ten
bonds, and pentavalent phosphorus), which realise \hivPairs{} degree
pairs; the count matrix has rank \hivRankM{} while the $18$-index
baseline has rank \hivRankB{}, so no baseline of $18$ BID indices can
certify this dataset. On the \hivRestrictedN{} HIV molecules of maximum
degree four the certificate holds again (ranks $10/10$, residual
$\hivRestrictedResid$). This is the intended behaviour: the data-free
certificate is a statement about $\Gc_4$, and the one-line data-level
rank check is what detects a dataset that leaves that class.

\input{tables_v2/general.tex}

\paragraph{A one-function implementation.} The certificate and the
coefficient vectors are available as a single function,
\texttt{certify(molecules, f, baseline)} in \texttt{src/certify.py},
which returns the data-free rank of $\Phi_B$, the realised pairs, the
data-level ranks and residuals, and, for a candidate $f$, its
coefficient vector on the baseline with the identity error on the
data. For the Euler Sombor index on five test molecules the identity
error is $7\times10^{-16}$; for the hyper-Zagreb index on the
ten-index basis it returns $HM=F+2M_2$ exactly. The $18\times10$ value
matrix $\Phi_B$ is listed in the Supplementary Information (Table~S2).

\section{Census of published degree-based indices}\label{sec:census}
% =====================================================================

We screened the $15$ instances of $14$ published BID indices in
Table~\ref{tab:census} --- eight Sombor-type instances (the $p$-Sombor
index at two values of $p$) and seven other degree-based indices ---
and all $100$ points of the Loyola grid
$(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in\{-1,-\tfrac12,0,\tfrac12,1\}^2\times\{0,\tfrac12,1,2\}$
of $LO(G;\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=\sum_{uv}(d_ud_v)^\alpha(d_u+d_v)^\beta
\exp\bigl(\gamma\,|d_u-d_v|/(d_u+d_v)\bigr)$~\cite{Paper1_Loyola} on each
of the four datasets. As controls outside the BID class we added the
Sombor coindex (sum over non-adjacent pairs) and the eccentric Sombor
index (eccentricities in place of degrees).

\input{tables_v2/census.tex}

Every published BID index lies in the span of the baseline to a relative
residual of at most $4\times10^{-13}$, and every Loyola grid point to
at most $2\times10^{-12}$; the two controls have relative residuals
of $0.006$--$0.008$ (coindex) and $0.06$--$0.08$ (eccentric Sombor),
i.e.\ they are correctly \emph{not} certified. The universal
coefficients of Corollary~\ref{cor:datafree} reproduce the
data-level values of all $15$ instances to relative error below
$10^{-10}$ (file \texttt{results/bid\_universal\_identities.csv}).
Two of them reduce to familiar identities, $GG_1 = M_1+M_2$ and
$HM = F+2M_2$; the others have non-sparse coefficient vectors, which
is the generic situation and the reason the redundancy is invisible
to a pairwise inspection.

The census is a numerical verification of
Corollary~\ref{cor:datafree}, not independent evidence for it. The
pairwise screen happens to reject all $15$ published instances
here (their maximal correlation with a baseline index is $0.98$--$1.00$),
and it passes only one of the $400$ Loyola evaluations ($100$ grid points
on four datasets) (FreeSolv,
$(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=(-1,-1,1)$, $\max|r|=0.943$). The certificate
reaches the same verdicts for a different reason: it is exact, needs
no threshold, and would apply equally to a variant whose pairwise
correlations happened to fall below any threshold.

\section{A numerical pitfall}\label{sec:pitfall}
% =====================================================================

An earlier draft of this work reported maximal partial correlations of
$0.028$ (ESOL), $0.036$ (FreeSolv) and $0.009$ (Lipophilicity) for the
Loyola grid. By Proposition~\ref{prop:cert} the true value is zero.
The nonzero values arose because a residual was declared zero only
when its absolute standard deviation fell below $10^{-12}$; for
indices of magnitude $10^2$--$10^4$ the least-squares residual of an
exactly redundant index is floating-point noise of relative size
$10^{-15}$--$10^{-12}$ but absolute size above that tolerance, and correlating
this noise with the target residual gives values of order
$1/\sqrt{n}$. Partial correlations are the most affected,
because they are a ratio of two noise quantities; VIF and incremental
$R^2$ computed in the same way appear as finite but meaningless numbers
(very large, respectively very small). We recommend
(a) a relative criterion $\|r_z\|/\|z-\bar z\|<10^{-8}$ with a
rank-revealing solver, (b) reporting the in-span status explicitly,
and (c) for BID candidates, replacing the statistical computation by
the certificate. Our implementation
(\texttt{src/orthogonality.py}, \texttt{span\_certified\_pcor}) does
all three; with it, all $400$ Loyola evaluations are exactly $0$.

\section{A target-aware screen for indices outside the BID class}\label{sec:nonbid}
% =====================================================================

For a candidate $z$ that is not certified, the question is whether it
carries information about a target $y$ beyond the baseline $X$. We
report three quantities.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \emph{Pairwise screen}: $\max_b|r(z,X_b)|<0.95$, kept only for
comparability with current practice.
\item \emph{Linear partial correlation with a confidence interval}:
$\pcor(z,y\mid X)$ from OLS residuals on $[\mathbf 1,X]$, with a
Fisher-$z$ interval with standard error $1/\sqrt{n-\operatorname{rk}X-3}$,
where $\operatorname{rk}X$ is the numerical rank of $X$, read against an
effect-size margin $\tau=0.10$ fixed before the analysis: \emph{pass}
if the $95\%$ interval for $|\pcor|$ lies above $\tau$,
\emph{negligible} if it lies below, \emph{inconclusive} otherwise.
The margin is a convention for a ``one percent of residual variance''
effect, not a significance threshold.
\item \emph{Non-linear conditional association}: $z$ and $y$ are each
residualized on $X$ by cross-fitted random forests (five folds), and
the correlation $\rho_{\mathrm{nl}}$ of the two residual vectors is
reported with its Fisher-$z$ interval, in the spirit of
double machine learning and the generalised covariance
measure~\cite{Chernozhukov2018DML,ShahPeters2020GCM}. Because the
forests are only approximate, we calibrate against three references
computed in the same way: an exactly redundant BID index, a pure-noise
column, and a deliberately leaky column $y+\varepsilon$.
\end{enumerate}

The $20$ candidates span information-theoretic, centrality,
spectral-hybrid, motif and eccentricity families
(Supplementary Information, Section~S3); none is claimed as new, and none is
a BID index. (Harmonic centrality is identically twice the Harary
index and is correctly flagged as in span.)

\input{tables_v2/nonbid.tex}

\paragraph{Results.} The pairwise screen admits $15$, $15$, $16$ and
$17$ of the $20$ candidates on ESOL, FreeSolv, Lipophilicity and BBBP.
Point estimates $|\pcor|\ge0.10$ would add four ESOL candidates and
one FreeSolv candidate, but with intervals none of them clears the
margin: they are inconclusive, with intervals straddling $0.10$
(Table~\ref{tab:nonbid}). This is largely a matter of power: the
half-width of a $95\%$ interval is about $0.08$ at $n=639$ (FreeSolv)
and $0.06$ at $n=1127$ (ESOL), so a pass would require $|\pcor|$ of
about $0.18$ and $0.16$. On Lipophilicity ($n=4200$) $18$ of the $20$
are negligible. Only on BBBP do two candidates, the triangle and
four-cycle counts, clear the margin with their whole interval
(linear-probability partial correlations $0.225$ and $-0.220$).

The non-linear check separates these further. The calibration
references behave as intended: the leaky column gives
$\rho_{\mathrm{nl}}=0.57$--$0.66$ on every dataset, while the exactly
redundant reference and the noise column stay small. Because the
random-forest residualization is not perfect --- on Lipophilicity even
the two null references have intervals that exclude zero --- we drew a
null distribution for $\rho_{\mathrm{nl}}$ with the same procedure:
\nullKB{} random BID indices $Mc$, $c\sim N(0,I)$, which are exactly in
the linear span of the baseline, and \nullKA{} pure-noise columns, per
dataset. The result is sobering. The $99$th percentile of
$|\rho_{\mathrm{nl}}|$ under the in-span null is $\nullBEsol$ (ESOL),
$\nullBFsv$ (FreeSolv), $\nullBLipo$ (Lipophilicity) and $\nullBBbbp$
(BBBP) --- the noise null gives $\nullAEsol$, $\nullAFsv$, $\nullALipo$
and $\nullABbbp$ --- so the residualization leaks structure of order
$0.1$ for columns that lie in the span but are hard for a forest to
fit, and any ``robust signal'' must be judged against that floor
rather than against zero (Figure~\ref{fig:screen}b). Two
candidate--dataset pairs exceed every one of the \nullKB{} exactly
redundant draws on their dataset, the degree entropy
$\mathrm{InfoH\_deg}$ on ESOL ($\rho_{\mathrm{nl}}=0.14$, $[0.08,0.20]$)
and the four-cycle count on BBBP ($-0.17$, $[-0.21,-0.13]$); with
\nullKB{} draws the smallest attainable empirical $p$-value is
$\nullPmin$, and after Benjamini--Hochberg adjustment over the $80$
pairs $q=\qInfoEsol$ for both, so \nullSurvivors{} pairs are significant
at $q<0.05$. The two are suggestive at the resolution of this test, no
more. Several linear ``signals'' vanish once the baseline is allowed to
act non-linearly --- most strikingly the BBBP triangle count
($0.225\to0.013$) --- which means that their linear residual
association was information the baseline already carries in
non-linear form. Permutation $p$-values of a distance correlation
between the residuals were not calibrated (the exactly redundant
reference was rejected on two datasets) and are not used.

\paragraph{Against a chemistry-aware baseline.} A QSPR practitioner's
baseline is not thirty topological indices but a standard descriptor
set. We therefore repeated the linear screen with the baseline
extended by RDKit 2D descriptors (the $180$ of Section~\ref{sec:ceiling},
of which $149$--$162$ remain after removing non-finite, constant and
collinear columns; none of the $30$ topological indices is collinear
with them) (Table~\ref{tab:chem}). Against the union baseline
\chemPassTotal{} of the $20$ candidates passes on any dataset. The two
candidates with the largest statistics above become negligible; on
Lipophilicity and BBBP most candidates are negligible, whereas on ESOL
and FreeSolv most remain unresolved at these sample sizes (interval
half-widths of about $0.06$ and $0.09$ with a baseline of rank about

negligible''. The reverse question has
almost the same answer: of the $30$ topological indices,

descriptors per dataset retain such signal given the topological
block. Out of sample, adding all $30$ indices and all $20$ candidates to
the RDKit descriptors improves a ridge model by at most
$\chemCvGainMax$ RMSE (ESOL; $3\%$ of the reference, a point estimate
from a single $5$-fold split without an interval). Topology-only
indices, new or old, add little to a chemistry-aware baseline on these
targets.

\section{The information ceiling of topology-only descriptors}\label{sec:ceiling}
% =====================================================================

Every descriptor considered in this paper --- all $30$ baseline indices,
all BID indices, and all $20$ alternative candidates --- is a function
of the hydrogen-suppressed, atom- and bond-unlabelled molecular graph.
Molecules with the same graph therefore receive identical descriptor
vectors, whatever their atoms, bonds or stereochemistry. We grouped the
curated molecules by a Weisfeiler--Lehman hash~\cite{Shervashidze2011WL}
of this graph (a canonical label built by four rounds of
neighbourhood-label aggregation, identical for isomorphic graphs), and confirmed with an exact isomorphism test that
every one of the $841$ groups of molecules sharing a hash consists of
isomorphic graphs; the grouping is therefore exact.

The collisions are extensive (Table~\ref{tab:data}): $55.5\%$ of the
curated ESOL molecules and $74.3\%$ of the FreeSolv molecules share their
graph with at least one other molecule; FreeSolv's $639$ molecules have
only $236$ distinct graphs, and the largest group contains $24$
molecules. Within groups of molecules that share a graph, the pooled
standard deviation of the target is $1.29$ (ESOL), $3.18$ (FreeSolv)
and $0.62$ (Lipophilicity), against overall standard deviations of
$2.09$, $3.85$ and $1.20$; on BBBP, $15.6\%$ of the shared groups
contain both labels. Because no topology-only model can separate
molecules within a group, this within-group variance cannot be reduced
by any such model (it also contains experimental noise, which limits
every model). Note that the pooled within-group standard deviation is
not the oracle's error: molecules with a unique graph contribute zero
within-group variance, which is why the oracle RMSE below is smaller.
The sharpest statement uses the \emph{graph oracle}: the
in-sample predictor that assigns every molecule the mean target of its
isomorphism class. No function of the unlabelled graph can beat it on
the same data. Its $R^2$ is $\oracleRsqEsol$ (ESOL), $\oracleRsqFsv$
(FreeSolv) and $\oracleRsqLipo$ (Lipophilicity), with RMSE
$\oracleRmseEsol$, $\oracleRmseFsv$ and $\oracleRmseLipo$
(Figure~\ref{fig:ceiling}); it is an in-sample bound for a dataset,
not a population quantity, and it is loose where most molecules have a
unique graph (Lipophilicity: $28\%$ shared). The out-of-sample
counterpart --- predicting each molecule from the other members of its
class, and by the global mean when it has none --- reaches only RMSE
$\looRmseEsol$, $\looRmseFsv$ and $\looRmseLipo$, which shows that
most of the information in these benchmarks is \emph{not} carried by
graph identity alone. The oracle nevertheless settles the FreeSolv
question: the best topology-only model (Table~\ref{tab:bench}) explains
far less than the oracle allows, and the chemistry-aware reference
below explains more out of sample ($R^2\approx\rdkitRsqFsv$, computed
as $1-\mathrm{RMSE}^2/\mathrm{Var}(y)$ from Table~\ref{tab:bench}) than
any topology-only model could in sample ($\oracleRsqFsv$); the same
holds on ESOL ($\rdkitRsqEsol$ versus $\oracleRsqEsol$). The
comparison is fair in the direction that matters: with folds grouped by
graph, the cross-validated predictions of any topology-only model are
constant within an isomorphism class, so its cross-validated error is
at least the in-sample oracle's. The weak
performance of topological indices on FreeSolv is therefore a property
of the representation, not of any particular index.

\paragraph{Benchmark.} To place the indices against this ceiling we
ran a downstream benchmark on the curated data (Table~\ref{tab:bench}).
Folds are grouped by graph so that identical descriptor vectors never
appear on both sides of a split; the design is $5\times5$ repeated
cross-validation; standardisation, screening, and the choice of the
LASSO penalty all happen inside the training folds; and paired
differences are tested with the Nadeau--Bengio variance
correction~\cite{NadeauBengio2003}, with equivalence assessed by two
one-sided tests (TOST)~\cite{Schuirmann1987TOST} against a margin of
$5\%$ of the reference RMSE, or $0.05\,(\mathrm{AUC}_{\mathrm{ref}}-0.5)$
for BBBP; the two one-sided tests at level $0.05$ correspond to the
reported $90\%$ intervals. As a chemistry-aware
reference we used a random forest on RDKit two-dimensional descriptors
($180$ of RDKit's $208$).\footnote{The remaining $28$ (EState- and
VSA-EState-type descriptors, BalabanJ, BertzCT and Ipc) terminate the
process under the RDKit~2022.09 / NumPy~2 combination used here and
were excluded. Three of them are topological; their absence can only
weaken the reference, which nevertheless leads by $0.37$--$2.18$ RMSE.}

Four results stand out. (i)~The chemistry-aware reference is far ahead
of every topology-only model --- by $0.81$ RMSE on ESOL, $2.18$ on
FreeSolv, $0.37$ on Lipophilicity and $0.10$ AUC on BBBP --- and on
ESOL and FreeSolv it exceeds, out of sample, the in-sample graph oracle
of the topology-only representation.
(ii)~In a random forest the ten counts $m_{ij}$ alone are not
distinguishable from the full $30$-index baseline on ESOL, FreeSolv and
BBBP (intervals include zero, although on FreeSolv the interval is
wide); only on Lipophilicity do
the distance and spectral indices add measurably ($+0.064$ RMSE for the
counts alone). (iii)~Restricting the baseline to its pairwise-screened subset, and the combined screen, are discussed in Supplementary Section~S4.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{Practical guidance.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Proposing a new degree-based index:} report its weight
vector $(f(i,j))_{(i,j)\in P_4}$ and its coefficients on a classical
basis (\texttt{certify}); do not report pairwise correlations as
evidence of novelty; justify the index by parsimony, interpretation or
mathematics, and compare its single-index $R^2$ with ordinary least
squares on the ten counts.
\item \emph{Modelling with topological descriptors:} use the ten counts
$m_{ij}$ directly instead of many BID indices; expect topology-only
descriptors to add little to a chemistry-aware descriptor set
($\le\chemCvGainMax$ RMSE here).
\item \emph{When the result does not apply:} indices outside the BID
class (Remark~\ref{rem:scope}), datasets with atoms of degree above four
(Section~\ref{sec:general}), and non-linear models, for which only the
empirical screen of Section~\ref{sec:nonbid} speaks.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{A concrete benchmark for BID indices.} Because every BID
index lies in $\col[\mathbf 1,M]$, its in-sample $R^2$ in a
single-index linear model can never exceed the $R^2$ of ordinary least
squares on the ten counts $m_{ij}$. On our data that bound is $0.42$
(ESOL), $0.19$ (FreeSolv), $0.12$ (Lipophilicity) and $0.26$ (BBBP, as
a linear probability model), while the best of the $18$ baseline
indices, the $15$ published instances and the $100$ Loyola points
reaches $0.36$, $0.13$, $0.05$ and $0.16$
(\texttt{results/bid\_r2\_bound.csv}). A proposer of a new BID index can
therefore report, on any dataset, how close the index comes to this
bound; a modeller who wants the degree information should simply use
the ten counts.

\paragraph{What a new degree-based index must show.} On molecular
graphs (maximum degree four) a new BID index cannot be linearly
independent of a baseline that includes ten generic classical
degree-based indices; this is a theorem, not an empirical finding.
Its justification must therefore come from elsewhere: parsimony and interpretability (one number that summarises the ten
counts for a given property; the companion study, still in
preparation, reports that its untuned point $LO(0,0,1)$, essentially the
irregularity index $IRLA$, matches its tuned models~\cite{Paper1_Loyola}),
mathematical properties, or
behaviour under models that are not linear in the descriptors. We
suggest that a proposal of a new BID index report its coefficient
vector $(f(i,j))_{(i,j)\in P_4}$, which places it exactly in the
common ten-dimensional space and makes coincidences between differently named indices immediately
visible.

\paragraph{What the target-aware screen adds.} For indices outside
the BID class the question is genuinely empirical, and the answer
depends on the baseline and on uncertainty. Against a chemistry-aware
baseline none passes (Table~\ref{tab:chem}); against the
topological baseline of the point-estimate ``passers'' in an
earlier version of this analysis, none survives on ESOL or FreeSolv
once intervals are reported, and most linear signals do not survive
non-linear residualization. A pairwise threshold, which ignores both
the multivariate baseline and the target, is not a meaningful
novelty criterion.

\paragraph{What topology cannot do.} Section~\ref{sec:ceiling} shows
that a large share of molecules in these benchmarks are
indistinguishable to every hydrogen-suppressed, unlabelled-graph
descriptor. This is not a defect of any particular index; it bounds
the whole class and explains the weak predictive performance of
topological indices on targets such as FreeSolv.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
% =====================================================================

\begin{itemize}
\item The certificate concerns linear redundancy; it does not rank
BID indices by usefulness, and it is silent on non-linear models
except through Section~\ref{sec:nonbid}.
\item The target-aware analyses use four MoleculeNet benchmarks. The
certificate itself was checked on thirteen; datasets with hypervalent
or metal centres (HIV) leave $\Gc_4$ and require either a larger
baseline or the counts $m_{ij}$ themselves, which the data-level rank
check of Proposition~\ref{prop:cert} detects.
\item The graph oracle is an in-sample bound for a given dataset; the
RDKit reference omits $28$ descriptors for software reasons
(footnote in Section~\ref{sec:ceiling}).
\item The effect-size margin $\tau=0.10$ is a convention; we report
intervals so that readers can apply their own.
\item The non-linear check relies on random-forest residualization,
whose null distribution for exactly redundant columns has a $99$th
percentile of $0.08$--$0.13$ depending on the dataset; effects below
that floor are not resolved, and with \nullKB{} draws per dataset the
smallest attainable $p$-value is \nullPmin.
\item BBBP partial correlations use a linear probability model.
\item The candidate set of Section~\ref{sec:nonbid} is illustrative,
not exhaustive.
\end{itemize}

% =====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% =====================================================================

For bond-incident-degree indices on molecular graphs, redundancy is
not a matter of thresholds: a rank condition on the baseline, met by
any ten generic classical degree-based indices, makes every such
index an exact linear combination of the baseline, with zero partial
correlation with every target. We verified this on \nGenDatasets{} MoleculeNet datasets
(\nGenMolecules{} molecules; the one exception contains organometallic
centres and is caught by the data-level check) and on fourteen
published indices ($15$ instances), including eight recent Sombor-type
variants, and showed that statistical diagnostics computed without this
knowledge can produce spurious signal. For indices outside the class, a
target-aware screen with intervals, a calibrated non-linear check and a
chemistry-aware baseline is informative, and on these benchmarks it
finds at most two suggestive signals against a topological baseline,
neither significant against a calibrated null, and none against a
chemistry-aware one. Both results sit under an information ceiling set by graph
collisions, which limits every topology-only descriptor.
