\begin{abstract}
On molecular graphs an edge has one of ten degree types, so every
bond-incident-degree index $\sum_{uv\in E} f(d_u,d_v)$ --- Zagreb,
Randi\'c, Sombor and their many variants --- is a linear combination of
the ten edge-type counts. We turn this classical fact into a
\emph{span certificate}: a rank condition on a baseline of classical
indices under which every such index, whatever its formula or
parameters, is an exact linear combination of the baseline and cannot
add linear information about any target. The certificate holds for a
baseline of eighteen classical degree-based indices on all molecular
graphs without hypervalent atoms.
We verify it across the MoleculeNet benchmarks, where the single
failure is diagnosed and recovered, and on a census of published
degree-based indices; the census also exposes a numerical pitfall that
turns exact zeros into spurious partial correlations. For indices
outside this class we give a target-aware screen with confidence
intervals and a non-linear check calibrated against deterministic
functions of the certified counts. The one residual signal that
survives those nulls does not survive a chemistry-aware baseline. A
last limit comes from graph collisions: hydrogen-suppressed graphs
coincide so often that, on two of the four benchmarks, every
topology-only descriptor is bounded in sample below what
chemistry-aware descriptors reach out of sample.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} Topological index; degree-based index;
Sombor index; redundancy; partial correlation; QSPR; molecular
descriptors.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Code and data.}
\url{https://github.com/Singati2/topological-index-orthogonality}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
% =====================================================================

Scalar topological indices have grown from a handful of early
proposals~\cite{Wiener1947,Gutman1972,Randic1975} to several hundred,
and the proliferation has been criticised within mathematical
chemistry itself~\cite{Gutman2013Degree}. The growth is fastest for
degree-based indices: since the Sombor index was introduced in
2021~\cite{Gutman2021Sombor}, modified, $p$-, KG-, elliptic, Euler,
diminished, hyperbolic, augmented, variable-Euler and cosine-rule Sombor
variants have followed
~\cite{KulliGutman2021Modified,GutmanFurtulaOz2024Elliptic,Gutman2024Euler,MovahediGutmanRedzepovicFurtula2026,BarmanDas2026Hyperbolic,RetiDoslicAli2021,KulliHarishChaluvarajuGutman2022KG,DasGutmanAli2026ASO,GutmanRedzepovicKizilirmakKulli2025Euler,NyauliBuragohain2026CoRSO},
and whole families of ``modified bond-based'' indices are being
benchmarked for QSPR~\cite{Altairi2026Modified,HayatAlanaziBelayWang2026}. A new index is commonly supported in two ways. One is
mathematical: bounds and extremal graphs. The other is a correlation
with a property on a small isomer set, usually accompanied by an
intercorrelation table against the existing
indices~\cite{Redzepovic2021ChemSombor,MovahediGutmanRedzepovicFurtula2026}.
Quality criteria that go beyond single correlations do exist:
structure sensitivity and abruptness~\cite{FurtulaGutmanDehmer2013},
applicability tests on octanes~\cite{KumarDas2024Structure},
clustering-based usefulness
scores~\cite{DehmerEmmertStreibTripathi2013,Ma2022Usefulness}. None
of them answers the question a QSPR modeller actually needs answered:
\emph{does the new index carry information about a target that an
explicit baseline does not already carry?}

For the largest class of indices this question is settled before any
data are examined. It is classical that a bond-incident-degree (BID)
index $I_f(G)=\sum_{uv\in E(G)} f(d_u,d_v)$ is the linear combination
$\sum_{i\le j} f(i,j)\,m_{ij}(G)$ of the edge-degree-pair counts
$m_{ij}$~\cite{Gutman2013Degree,RadaCruz2014VDB}. Rada and co-workers
have recently built a finite-dimensional linear theory of
vertex-degree-based indices on this
fact~\cite{CruzEspinalRada2024Matrix,Rada2026LinearVDB}, and they note
that the analytic form of $f$ is irrelevant once its values on the
degree pairs are fixed. Now suppose a baseline of classical
degree-based indices spans the edge-degree-pair counts realised in a
dataset. That is a rank condition, and it involves neither the
candidate nor the target. Then \emph{every} BID index, whatever its
functional form or parameters, is an exact linear combination of the
baseline on that dataset, and a target-aware redundancy screen has
nothing left to measure. We call this rank condition a \emph{span
certificate}: a baseline is \emph{certified} on a dataset when the
condition holds, and a candidate is then \emph{certified redundant}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item \emph{Span certificate} (Section~\ref{sec:theory}). The
mathematics is not ours: the linearity of BID indices in the counts
$m_{ij}$ and the finite-dimensional framework built on it are due to
Gutman~\cite{Gutman2013Degree}, Rada and
Cruz~\cite{RadaCruz2014VDB} and Rada~\cite{Rada2026LinearVDB}, and that
framework contains no statistical or dataset-level analysis. What is
ours is the statistical reading. We give an explicit, checkable rank
condition on a baseline under which every BID index has zero residual,
infinite variance inflation and no residual direction towards any
target (Proposition~\ref{prop:cert}), and its data-free form for
graphs of maximum degree at most four, with explicit coefficient
vectors (Corollary~\ref{cor:datafree}). Rada gives a data-free
realisation of any weight system by a symmetric polynomial; the
corollary instead gives the rank condition and the coefficient vectors
on a \emph{named} baseline of indices that are actually computed. We
then observe that an $18$-index baseline of classical degree-based
indices satisfies the condition, and verify it on benchmark data.
\item \emph{Generality} (Section~\ref{sec:general}). The certificate
is checked on \nGenDatasets{} MoleculeNet SMILES datasets
(\nGenMolecules{} molecules): it holds on \nGenCertified{}, and the one
failure (HIV) is caused by \hivHyper{} organometallic molecules with
atoms of degree five to ten --- the case the data-free certificate
excludes and the data-level check catches.
\item \emph{Census} (Section~\ref{sec:census}). \nCensusIdx{} published
degree-based indices (\nCensusInst{} instances, \nSomborInst{} of them
Sombor-type, including the 2025--2026 augmented, variable-Euler and
cosine-rule variants) and $100$ points of a three-parameter family, on
four MoleculeNet datasets: all redundant to relative residuals below
$10^{-11}$, as the corollary predicts, where earlier correlational
reports could only approximate; two non-BID Sombor-type controls
correctly fall outside the span.
\item \emph{A numerical pitfall and a tool} (Section~\ref{sec:pitfall}).
For exactly redundant indices, partial correlations computed with
absolute residual tolerances are noise of order $10^{-2}$; we give a
numerically safe recipe and a one-function implementation of the
certificate that returns each index's coefficient vector.
\item \emph{Target-aware screen for non-BID indices}
(Section~\ref{sec:nonbid}). Confidence intervals against an
effect-size margin and a cross-fitted non-linear residualization with
a proper null distribution, applied to $20$ alternative candidates;
the screen is then repeated against a chemistry-aware (RDKit 2D)
baseline.
\item \emph{Information ceiling} (Section~\ref{sec:ceiling}). Graph
collisions under hydrogen suppression, confirmed by exact isomorphism,
give an in-sample oracle for the whole topology-only representation; a
graph-grouped, corrected benchmark places the indices against this
oracle and against an atom- and bond-aware descriptor reference.
\end{enumerate}

One of the parametric families screened below, the three-parameter
Loyola family of Section~\ref{sec:census}, is studied mathematically in
separate work by a subset of the present authors. Here it serves only
as a generic parametric BID family. The span certificate explains why
no such family can add linearly to a classical baseline.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{Redundancy and orthogonalization.} Strong mutual
correlation among topological indices is long established. Hollas
studied the redundancy of graph invariants used in
chemoinformatics~\cite{Hollas2006Redundancy} and related the
covariance of degree-based indices to the edge structure of the
graph~\cite{Hollas2005Covariance}; orthogonalization of descriptors is
an equally old remedy~\cite{Lucic1997Orthogonal,Du2002Orthogonal}.
Recent work continues to report intercorrelations among new
variants, e.g.\ among Sombor-type indices on octanes~\cite{MovahediGutmanRedzepovicFurtula2026,Redzepovic2021ChemSombor},
and large-scale descriptor evaluations cluster descriptors without
reference to a target~\cite{DehmerEmmertStreibTripathi2013,Ma2022Usefulness}.
We do not claim to discover that topological indices are redundant.

\paragraph{Linear structure of degree-based indices.} The identity
$I_f=\sum f(i,j)m_{ij}$ is standard~\cite{Gutman2013Degree,RadaCruz2014VDB}
and underlies the bond-additive modelling programme in which the
term ``bond incident degree'' index originates~\cite{VukicevicGasperov2010Adriatic,VukicevicDjurdjevic2011BID}.
Matrix and finite-dimensional linear formulations of
vertex-degree-based indices, including their discrimination
properties, are developed in~\cite{CruzEspinalRada2024Matrix,Rada2026LinearVDB}.
The M-polynomial~\cite{DeutschKlavzar2015} is the generating function
of the same counts. Further algebraic constructions on degree-based
indices appear in~\cite{GutmanBuragohainNyauli2026Concealed}, and the
edge-type-count view underlies recent extremal-graph work for chemical
graphs of maximum degree
three~\cite{Bonte2025Extremal,Dusollier2025Polyhedral,Bonte2025ChemicHull}.
Contribution~(1) states what this paper takes from that literature
and what it adds.

Recent ``modified bond-based'' families that weight each edge by a
function of its end degrees~\cite{Altairi2026Modified} are BID indices in
the sense used here, so the certificate of Section~\ref{sec:theory}
applies to them unchanged.

\paragraph{Degeneracy.} That distinct graphs can share index values
(degeneracy) and that hydrogen suppression merges distinct molecules
are classical concerns~\cite{Konstantinova1996,Pogliani2008,Dehmer2012Degeneracy};
a recent audit of the MoleculeNet benchmarks documents duplicate and
conflicting entries at the SMILES level~\cite{SchuhDanilukSieber2026}.
Section~\ref{sec:ceiling} quantifies collisions at the level of the
hydrogen-suppressed graph (isomorphism classes), the level that
matters for topological indices, and uses them as a ceiling for the
whole topology-only representation rather than for a single index.
The idea of bounding accuracy by Weisfeiler--Lehman-indistinguishable
graphs with conflicting labels is due to Zopf~\cite{Zopf2022WL}, for
labelled graph benchmarks.

\paragraph{Descriptor evaluation in QSPR.} Topological descriptors
remain competitive baselines in modern property
prediction~\cite{AdamczykCzech2024MOLTOP,PraskiAdamczykCzech2025}, which keeps the question of
which indices are worth adding practically relevant. Generic feature
selection (LASSO~\cite{Tibshirani1996}, mRMR~\cite{PengLongDing2005},
Boruta~\cite{KursaRudnicki2010}) acts on a fixed descriptor matrix;
the screen here acts one layer earlier, on whether a proposed index
should enter that matrix at all.

\section{Data, curation and baseline}\label{sec:data}
% =====================================================================

We use four MoleculeNet benchmarks~\cite{MoleculeNet}: ESOL (aqueous
solubility)~\cite{Delaney2004ESOL}, FreeSolv (experimental hydration
free energy; column \texttt{expt})~\cite{Mobley2014FreeSolv},
Lipophilicity ($\log D_{7.4}$)~\cite{LipophilicityChEMBL} and BBBP
(blood--brain barrier penetration, binary)~\cite{Martins2012BBBP}.
SMILES are parsed with RDKit~\cite{RDKit} and the largest fragment is
kept. From it we form the hydrogen-suppressed, atom- and
bond-unlabelled graph, dropping molecules with fewer than two heavy
atoms, so that every graph is connected and has no isolated vertex. On
the files as distributed this leaves $1127$, $639$, $4200$ and $2039$
molecules (the ``parsed'' column of Table~\ref{tab:data}).
Stereochemistry and atom identity are invisible to every descriptor
studied here, so molecules that differ only in these respects become
duplicates. We report all screening results on the files as
distributed. Section~\ref{sec:nonbid} verifies that de-duplication on
canonical SMILES changes no conclusion (regression duplicates whose
labels agree within $0.5$ target units are averaged; groups that
disagree by more, or carry conflicting BBBP labels, are dropped). The
benchmark of Section~\ref{sec:ceiling} uses the curated data. All
molecules have maximum degree at most four after hydrogen suppression.

\paragraph{Baseline.} The $30$-index baseline (Supplementary Table~S1)
contains $18$ degree-based indices, $8$ distance-based indices,
$3$ spectral indices and the Balaban index. All $18$ degree-based
indices are BID indices in the sense of Section~\ref{sec:theory}
(vertex-degree sums $\sum_v g(d_v)$ are BID with
$f(a,b)=g(a)/a+g(b)/b$ on graphs without isolated vertices). Several
baseline members are related by exact identities, which we disclose
because they matter for numerical rank: $\sigma = F-2M_2$ and
$\mathrm{redM}_1 = M_1-4|E(G)|+|V(G)|$. The index
labelled ``Schultz'' in our code is the degree distance
$\sum_{\{u,v\}}(d_u+d_v)\,d(u,v)$ (the Schultz molecular topological
index differs by $M_1$); PI is the edge Padmakar--Ivan index; the
augmented Zagreb summand is set to zero on $K_2$ edges.

\section{The span certificate}\label{sec:theory}
% =====================================================================

Let $\Gc_\Delta$ be the class of finite simple graphs without
isolated vertices and with maximum degree at most $\Delta$, and
$P_\Delta=\{(i,j):1\le i\le j\le\Delta\}$, so $|P_4|=10$. For
$G\in\Gc_\Delta$ let $m_{ij}(G)$ be the number of edges whose end
degrees are $\{i,j\}$. A \emph{BID index} is
$I_f(G)=\sum_{uv\in E(G)} f(d_u,d_v)$ for a symmetric
$f:\{1,\dots,\Delta\}^2\to\mathbb{R}$.

\begin{remark}[classical]\label{rem:linear}
$I_f(G)=\sum_{(i,j)\in P_\Delta} f(i,j)\,m_{ij}(G)$, because the
edge classes $\{uv:\{d_u,d_v\}=\{i,j\}\}$ partition $E(G)$ and $f$ is
constant on each~\cite{Gutman2013Degree,RadaCruz2014VDB}. For example, the path on four vertices has two edges of type $(1,2)$
and one of type $(2,2)$, so $I_f=2f(1,2)+f(2,2)$ on it for every $f$, and the star
$K_{1,3}$ has three edges of type $(1,3)$, so $I_f(K_{1,3})=3f(1,3)$.
On $\Gc_4$ the ten functionals $m_{ij}$ are linearly independent: the complete
bipartite graph $K_{i,j}$ has all $ij$ edges of type $(i,j)$, so its
count vector is a multiple of the $(i,j)$-th unit vector. So the BID
indices on $\Gc_4$ form exactly a ten-dimensional space; on connected
graphs with at least three vertices $m_{11}=0$ and the space is at
most nine-dimensional.
\end{remark}

Now fix a dataset $D=\{G_1,\dots,G_n\}\subset\Gc_\Delta$ with
realised pair set $P_D\subseteq P_\Delta$, count matrix
$M\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times|P_D|}$, and a baseline whose BID block
consists of $k$ indices with value matrix
$\Phi_B\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times|P_D|}$, $(\Phi_B)_{b,(i,j)}=f_b(i,j)$. The
baseline's BID columns are $B=M\Phi_B^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}$.
Write $\mathbf 1$ for the all-ones vector.

The following is an elementary consequence of Remark~\ref{rem:linear}.
Its content lies in the statistical reading (i)--(iv).

\begin{proposition}[Span certificate]\label{prop:cert}
If $\operatorname{rank}[\mathbf 1, B]=\operatorname{rank}[\mathbf 1,M]$,
then for every symmetric $f$ the vector $z_f=(I_f(G_t))_{t\le n}$
lies in $\col[\mathbf 1,B]$. It follows that, for any baseline matrix
$X$ whose columns include $B$ and for every target vector $y$:
(i) the least-squares residual of $z_f$ on $[\mathbf 1,X]$ is zero;
(ii) if $z_f$ is not constant on $D$, its variance inflation relative to
$X$ is infinite;
(iii) the partial correlation $\pcor(z_f,y\mid X)$ --- the correlation
between the residuals of $z_f$ and of $y$ after least-squares regression
on $[\mathbf 1,X]$ --- is undefined ($0/0$):
$z_f$ carries no direction beyond $X$, and we report it as $0$ by
convention --- any nonzero value computed in floating point is an
artefact (Section~\ref{sec:pitfall}); and
(iv) adding $z_f$ to $X$ leaves every ordinary least-squares fit
(fitted values, residuals, $R^2$) on $D$ unchanged. Penalised linear
fits (ridge, LASSO) can change, but only through the geometry of the
penalty, not through any new direction in the data.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Remark~\ref{rem:linear}, $z_f=Mf_D$ with $f_D=(f(i,j))_{(i,j)\in P_D}$,
so $z_f\in\col(M)$. Since $B=M\Phi_B^{\top}$, $\col[\mathbf 1,B]\subseteq\col[\mathbf 1,M]$,
and equal ranks give equality. So $z_f\in\col[\mathbf 1,B]\subseteq\col[\mathbf 1,X]$,
which is (i); (ii) and (iii) restate (i), and (iv) holds because an
OLS fit depends on the design only through its column space.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[data-free certificate on $\Gc_4$]\label{cor:datafree}
If $\operatorname{rank}\Phi_B=10$ over $P_4$, then for every symmetric
$f$ there is a coefficient vector $c$, unique up to $\ker \Phi_B^{\top}$, with
$\Phi_B^{\top}c=f_{P_4}$, and $I_f(G)=\sum_b c_b\,I_{f_b}(G)$ holds
\emph{identically} on $\Gc_4$. The conclusions of
Proposition~\ref{prop:cert} then hold on every dataset of graphs in
$\Gc_4$, without inspecting the data.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$\operatorname{rank}\Phi_B=10$ means the rows $f_b$ span
$\mathbb{R}^{P_4}$; then
\[
I_f=\sum_{ij}f(i,j)\,m_{ij}=\sum_{ij}\sum_b c_b\, f_b(i,j)\, m_{ij}=\sum_b c_b\, I_{f_b}.
\]
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The baseline satisfies the certificate.} Evaluating each of
the $18$ degree-based baseline indices on $K_{i,j}$ gives $\Phi_B$
exactly (Supplementary Table~S2); its rank over $P_4$ is $10$ (smallest-to-largest singular
value ratio $1.2\times10^{-4}$), so Corollary~\ref{cor:datafree}
applies. The certificate is generic rather than delicate. Of the
\nAllSubsets{} ten-element subsets of the $18$ indices,
\nGenericSubsets{} already have rank $10$ at the relative
singular-value tolerance $10^{-9}$ used for every numerical rank in
this paper; the count moves by a few units with the tolerance. One
such subset is $\{M_1,M_2,mM_1,mM_2,F,R,\mathrm{SCI},H,GA,AG\}$. On
the data, the numerical ranks of $M$, $B$ and $[B,M]$ coincide on all
four datasets (Table~\ref{tab:cert}).

\begin{remark}[what the certificate does and does not say]\label{rem:scope}
The certificate is a statement about \emph{linear} redundancy. It
does not say that a BID index is useless: a parsimonious index can
summarise the ten counts well, and a model that is not
rotation-invariant (e.g.\ a tree ensemble) may use a BID index
differently from its constituents. It does say that no BID index can
add linearly to a certified baseline, so the multivariate linear
redundancy diagnostics (the variance inflation factor, the partial
correlation and the gain of an ordinary least-squares model) are
decided in advance for this class. Pairwise correlations with
individual baseline indices are not decided, which is why they cannot
detect this redundancy.
The certificate covers edge-additive indices only; it does not
cover normalised or averaged indices such as $I_f/|E|$, multiplicative
indices $\prod f$, non-linear transforms such as $\log I_f$, products
of indices, neighbourhood-degree-sum and other second-order indices,
or valence- and heteroatom-weighted degrees. Beyond $\Delta=4$
(hypervalent atoms, explicit hydrogens, coordination compounds) the space has dimension $\Delta(\Delta+1)/2$
and the certificate must be checked on the data, which
Proposition~\ref{prop:cert} makes a one-line rank computation.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Scope check across MoleculeNet}\label{sec:general}

The data-free certificate settles every dataset of graphs with maximum
degree four, so running the data-level check of
Proposition~\ref{prop:cert} on the \nGenDatasets{} SMILES-based
MoleculeNet datasets (\nGenMolecules{} molecules; Supplementary
Table~S6) is informative only where molecules leave that class. Eight
datasets, including QM9 and MUV, contain none and pass as the corollary
requires. Four (ClinTox, SIDER, Tox21, ToxCast) contain one to three
molecules with an atom of degree five or six, and there certification
depends on the data. As functions on $P_6$ the $18$ baseline functions
have rank only $17$ ($\sigma=F-2M_2$ exactly), so at most seven pairs
beyond $P_4$ can ever be absorbed. These datasets realise one to three
such pairs and pass. HIV fails. \hivHyper{} of its $41\,127$ molecules
are organometallic complexes (Co, Ni, Cu, Rh, Fe, Mn, Cr, Mo, Ir
centres with five to ten bonds, and pentavalent phosphorus). With
them the dataset realises \hivPairs{} degree pairs, more than any $18$
functions can span (ranks \hivRankM{} versus \hivRankB{}). On its \hivRestrictedN{}
molecules of maximum degree four the certificate holds again. The
practical content of the check is the diagnosis: a dataset with hypervalent or metal centres needs the
counts $m_{ij}$ themselves, or a larger baseline, before any BID index
can be certified.

\paragraph{A one-function implementation.} The certificate and the
coefficient vectors are available as a single function,
\texttt{certify(molecules, f, baseline)} in \texttt{src/certify.py}.
It returns the data-free rank of $\Phi_B$, the realised pairs and the
data-level ranks and residuals. For a candidate $f$ it also returns
the coefficient vector on the baseline, with the identity error on the
data. For the Euler Sombor index on five test molecules the identity
error is $7\times10^{-16}$; for the hyper-Zagreb index on the
ten-index basis it returns $HM=F+2M_2$ exactly. The $18\times10$ value
matrix $\Phi_B$ is listed in the Supplementary Information (Table~S2).

\section{Census of published degree-based indices}\label{sec:census}
% =====================================================================

Table~\ref{tab:census} lists the \nCensusInst{} instances of
\nCensusIdx{} published BID indices that we screened: \nSomborInst{}
Sombor-type instances (the $p$-Sombor index at two values of $p$; the
augmented, cosine-rule and variable-Euler variants of
2025--2026~\cite{DasGutmanAli2026ASO,NyauliBuragohain2026CoRSO,GutmanRedzepovicKizilirmakKulli2025Euler})
and seven other degree-based indices. To these we added all $100$
points of the Loyola grid
$(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in\{-1,-\tfrac12,0,\tfrac12,1\}^2\times\{0,\tfrac12,1,2\}$
of $LO(G;\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=\sum_{uv}(d_ud_v)^\alpha(d_u+d_v)^\beta
\exp\bigl(\gamma\,|d_u-d_v|/(d_u+d_v)\bigr)$, a three-parameter BID
family that reduces to the Gutman--Milovanovi\'c
family~\cite{GutmanMilovanovic2020AKCE} at $\gamma=0$.
Everything was evaluated on each of the four datasets. As controls
outside the BID class we added the Sombor coindex (sum over
non-adjacent pairs) and the eccentric Sombor index (eccentricities in
place of degrees).

\input{tables_v2/census.tex}

Every published BID index lies in the span of the baseline to a relative
residual of at most $4\times10^{-13}$, and every Loyola grid point to
at most $2\times10^{-12}$. The two controls have relative residuals
of $0.006$--$0.008$ (coindex) and $0.06$--$0.08$ (eccentric Sombor),
so they are correctly \emph{not} certified. The universal
coefficients of Corollary~\ref{cor:datafree} reproduce the
data-level values of all \nCensusInst{} instances to relative error
below $10^{-10}$ (column \texttt{identity\_rel\_error\_on\_data} of
\texttt{results/bid\_variant\_screen.csv}).
Two of them reduce to familiar identities, $GG_1 = M_1+M_2$ and
$HM = F+2M_2$; the others have non-sparse coefficient vectors, which
is the generic situation and the reason the redundancy is invisible
to a pairwise inspection.

The census is a numerical verification of
Corollary~\ref{cor:datafree}, not independent evidence for it. The
pairwise screen happens to reject all \nCensusInst{} published instances
here (their maximal correlation with a baseline index is $0.98$--$1.00$),
and it passes only one of the $400$ Loyola evaluations ($100$ grid points
on four datasets) (FreeSolv,
$(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=(-1,-1,1)$, $\max|r|=0.943$). The certificate
reaches the same verdicts for a different reason. It is exact and
needs no threshold, and it would apply unchanged to a variant whose
pairwise correlations happened to fall below any threshold.

\section{A numerical pitfall (remark)}\label{sec:pitfall}
% =====================================================================

An earlier draft of this work reported maximal partial correlations of
$0.028$ (ESOL), $0.036$ (FreeSolv) and $0.009$ (Lipophilicity) for the
Loyola grid. By Proposition~\ref{prop:cert} these quantities are
undefined and are reported as zero.
The nonzero values arose because a residual was declared zero only
when its absolute standard deviation fell below $10^{-12}$. For
indices of magnitude $10^2$--$10^4$ the least-squares residual of an
exactly redundant index is floating-point noise of relative size
$10^{-15}$--$10^{-12}$, which in absolute terms sits above that
tolerance. Correlating this noise with the target residual gives
values of order $1/\sqrt{n}$. Partial correlations are the most affected,
because they are a ratio of two noise quantities; VIF and incremental
$R^2$ computed in the same way appear as finite but meaningless numbers
(very large, respectively very small). We recommend
(a) a relative criterion $\|r_z\|/\|z-\bar z\|<10^{-8}$ with a
rank-revealing solver (numerical ranks themselves use a relative
singular-value tolerance of $10^{-9}$; the two tolerances play
different roles), (b) reporting the in-span status explicitly,
and (c) for BID candidates, replacing the statistical computation by
the certificate. Our implementation
(\texttt{src/orthogonality.py}, \texttt{span\_certified\_pcor}) does
all three; with it, all $400$ Loyola evaluations are exactly $0$.

\section{A target-aware screen for indices outside the BID class}\label{sec:nonbid}
% =====================================================================

For a candidate $z$ that is not certified, the question is whether it
carries information about a target $y$ beyond the baseline $X$. We
report three quantities.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \emph{Pairwise screen}: $\max_b|r(z,X_b)|<0.95$, kept only for
comparability with current practice.
\item \emph{Linear partial correlation with a confidence interval}:
$\pcor(z,y\mid X)$ from OLS residuals on $[\mathbf 1,X]$, with a
Fisher-$z$ interval with standard error $1/\sqrt{n-\operatorname{rk}X-3}$,
where $\operatorname{rk}X$ is the numerical rank of $X$, read against an
effect-size margin $\tau=0.10$ fixed before the analysis: \emph{pass}
if the $95\%$ interval for $|\pcor|$ lies above $\tau$,
\emph{negligible} if it lies below, \emph{inconclusive} otherwise.
The margin is a convention for a ``one percent of residual variance''
effect, not a significance threshold.
\item \emph{Non-linear conditional association}: $z$ and $y$ are each
residualized on $X$ by cross-fitted random forests (five folds), and
the correlation $\rho_{\mathrm{nl}}$ of the two residual vectors is
reported with its Fisher-$z$ interval, in the spirit of
double machine learning and the generalised covariance
measure~\cite{Chernozhukov2018DML,ShahPeters2020GCM}. Because the
forests are only approximate, we calibrate against three references
computed in the same way: an exactly redundant BID index, a pure-noise
column, and a deliberately leaky column $y+\varepsilon$.
\end{enumerate}

The $20$ candidates span information-theoretic, centrality,
spectral-hybrid, motif and eccentricity families
(Supplementary Information, Section~S3). None is claimed as a newly
invented index (two product indices are constructed here as controls),
and none is a BID index. (Harmonic centrality is identically twice the Harary
index and is correctly flagged as in span.)

\input{tables_v2/nonbid.tex}

\paragraph{Results.} The pairwise screen admits $15$, $15$, $16$ and
$17$ of the $20$ candidates on ESOL, FreeSolv, Lipophilicity and BBBP.
Point estimates $|\pcor|\ge0.10$ would add four ESOL candidates and
one FreeSolv candidate, but with intervals none of them clears the
margin: they are inconclusive, with intervals straddling $0.10$
(Table~\ref{tab:nonbid}). This is largely a matter of power: the
half-width of a $95\%$ interval is about $0.08$ at $n=639$ (FreeSolv)
and $0.06$ at $n=1127$ (ESOL), so a pass would require $|\pcor|$ of
about $0.18$ and $0.16$. On Lipophilicity ($n=4200$) $18$ of the $20$
are negligible. Only on BBBP do two candidates, the triangle and
four-cycle counts, clear the margin with their whole interval
(linear-probability partial correlations $0.225$ and $-0.220$).

The non-linear check is the weakest part of the screen, and it ends
in a withdrawal. The calibration references behave as intended:
the leaky column gives $\rho_{\mathrm{nl}}=0.57$--$0.66$ on every
dataset, while the exactly redundant reference and the noise column
stay small. Because the random-forest residualization is not perfect,
we drew three null distributions with the same procedure, per dataset.
Null~B: \nullKB{} random BID indices $Mc$, $c\sim N(0,I)$, exactly in
the linear span of the baseline. Null~C: \nullKC{} deterministic
non-linear functions $\phi(Mc)$ of such columns. Null~D: \nullKD{}
entropy-type functions of the degree-count vector. Null~D is the class
the degree entropy itself belongs to, since the counts $n_1,\dots,n_4$
are linear in the $m_{ij}$ ($k\,n_k=\sum_{j}m_{kj}+m_{kk}$) and so lie
in the span of the baseline. The $99$th percentile of
$|\rho_{\mathrm{nl}}|$ is $\nullBEsol$ / $\nullCEsol$ / $\nullDEsol$
(ESOL), $\nullBFsv$ / $\nullCFsv$ / $\nullDFsv$ (FreeSolv),
$\nullBLipo$ / $\nullCLipo$ / $\nullDLipo$ (Lipophilicity) and
$\nullBBbbp$ / $\nullCBbbp$ / $\nullDBbbp$ (BBBP) under nulls B / C / D.
So a column that carries no information beyond the baseline can show
$|\rho_{\mathrm{nl}}|$ up to about $0.17$, for no better reason than
that a forest residualizes a smooth function of ten counts
imperfectly. Two candidate--dataset pairs exceed the linear null~B.
The first is the degree entropy $\mathrm{InfoH\_deg}$ on ESOL
($\rho_{\mathrm{nl}}=0.14$): $p=\pInfoEsol$ against null~B and
$p=\pCInfoEsol$ against null~C, but $p=\pDInfoEsol$ against null~D.
Null~D was constructed after this value was seen, and we say so; its
justification is not post hoc, because the degree entropy is provably
a deterministic function of the certified counts (the identity above),
so a null of exactly that class is the right comparison, and inside it
the value is unremarkable. What looked like residual association is
approximation error. The second is the four-cycle count on BBBP
($-0.17$, $[-0.21,-0.13]$), which is not a function of the baseline
(it needs $\operatorname{tr}A^4$) and exceeds every draw of nulls B,
C and D ($p=\pFourBbbp$, $\pCFourBbbp$ and $\pDFourBbbp$). After
Benjamini--Hochberg adjustment over the $80$ pairs (the four in-span
harmonic-centrality pairs included) it reaches $q=\qFourBbbp$ against
null~B and $q=0.4$ and $0.2$ against nulls C and D, and it is negligible
against the chemistry-aware baseline below (partial correlation
$-0.02$ given RDKit descriptors alone). The degree entropy on FreeSolv
($p=\pInfoFsv$ against null~B) is the only other pair below $0.05$
on any null, and it is inside nulls C and D. (The
empirical $p$-values use the $100$-tree statistic of the null runs;
Table~\ref{tab:nonbid} reports the $300$-tree values, which differ by at
most $0.004$; the Fisher intervals shown for $\rho_{\mathrm{nl}}$ are
descriptive.) Several linear ``signals'' vanish once the baseline is
allowed to act non-linearly; the BBBP triangle count is the clearest
case ($0.225\to0.013$). The natural reading is that the baseline
already carries that information in non-linear form, but we have not
calibrated how much of a true signal the residualizer attenuates, so
we do not press the point.

We draw a plain conclusion from this. With one learner (a random
forest, five folds) and $n$ between $639$ and $4200$, the
residualization is not accurate enough to certify non-linear
redundancy: the theory behind the generalised covariance measure
requires the nuisance regressions to be accurate, and here they are
not, which is what the nulls measure. Our own earlier draft reported
the ESOL degree entropy as a suggestive signal on exactly this basis,
and that reading was wrong. The exact certificate and the
chemistry-aware baseline, not this check, carry the conclusions of
the paper. Permutation $p$-values of a distance correlation between
the residuals were not calibrated either (the exactly redundant
reference was rejected on two datasets) and are not used.

\paragraph{Against a chemistry-aware baseline.} A QSPR practitioner's
baseline is not thirty topological indices but a standard descriptor
set. So we repeated the screen with RDKit 2D descriptors (the $180$ of
Section~\ref{sec:ceiling}), on the curated data and with the hygiene
of the benchmark: folds grouped by isomorphism class, with removal of
non-finite, constant and collinear columns, scaling and penalty
selection all inside the training folds (Table~\ref{tab:chem}). Two
baselines are informative. Given RDKit descriptors \emph{alone},
\chemPassRdTotal{} candidate--dataset pairs pass the interval screen
(\chemPassRdEsol{} on ESOL, \chemPassRdFsv{} on FreeSolv, none
elsewhere). The passers are distance-based and eccentricity-based
information indices, plus the two product controls; they carry signal
that RDKit's descriptor set does not. Given RDKit descriptors
\emph{and} the $30$ classical indices, \chemPassTotal{} pairs pass.
The classical indices absorb that signal; we did not isolate which of
them do so, but the passers are themselves distance- and
eccentricity-based, like the classical Wiener, Harary and Estrada
indices, and among the $30$ only the Mostar index on ESOL passes the
same interval screen against RDKit alone. On ESOL and
FreeSolv most verdicts become inconclusive at these sample sizes
(\chemIncEsol{} and \chemIncFsv{} of $20$) rather than negligible. Out
of sample the verdict does not change. Adding the $30$ indices and the
$20$ candidates to the RDKit descriptors changes grouped
cross-validated RMSE by at most $\chemCvGainMax$ (ESOL), makes
Lipophilicity slightly worse ($+\chemCvLossLipo$), and changes BBBP's
AUC by $\chemCvAucGain$. Two practical points follow. The classical
distance and spectral indices earn their place beside a
chemistry-aware descriptor set only where they fill a gap in it. And a
new topology-only index that passes against RDKit alone should still
be checked against the classical indices before anyone calls it
novel.

\section{The information ceiling of topology-only descriptors}\label{sec:ceiling}
% =====================================================================

Every descriptor in this paper, the $30$ baseline indices, the BID
indices and the $20$ alternative candidates alike, is a function of
the hydrogen-suppressed, atom- and bond-unlabelled molecular graph.
Molecules with the same graph receive identical descriptor vectors,
whatever their atoms, bonds or stereochemistry. We grouped the curated
molecules by a Weisfeiler--Lehman hash~\cite{Shervashidze2011WL} of
this graph, an isomorphism invariant built by four rounds of
neighbourhood-label aggregation.
An exact isomorphism test then confirmed that every one of the $841$
groups of molecules sharing a hash consists of isomorphic graphs. The
grouping is exact.

The collisions are extensive (Table~\ref{tab:data}): $55.5\%$ of the
curated ESOL molecules and $74.3\%$ of the FreeSolv molecules share their
graph with at least one other molecule; FreeSolv's $639$ molecules have
only $236$ distinct graphs, and the largest group contains $24$
molecules. Within groups of molecules that share a graph, the pooled
standard deviation of the target is $1.29$ (ESOL), $3.18$ (FreeSolv)
and $0.62$ (Lipophilicity). The full-sample standard deviations are
$2.09$, $3.85$ and $1.20$. On BBBP, $15.6\%$ of the shared groups
contain both labels. No topology-only model can separate molecules
within a group, so this within-group variance cannot be reduced by
any such model; it also contains experimental noise, which limits
every model. The pooled within-group standard deviation is not the
oracle's error, though. Molecules with a unique graph contribute zero
within-group variance, which is why the oracle RMSE below is smaller.
The sharpest statement uses the \emph{graph oracle}: the
in-sample predictor that assigns every molecule the mean target of its
isomorphism class. No function of the unlabelled graph can beat it on
the same data. Its $R^2$ is $\oracleRsqEsol$ (ESOL), $\oracleRsqFsv$
(FreeSolv) and $\oracleRsqLipo$ (Lipophilicity), with RMSE
$\oracleRmseEsol$, $\oracleRmseFsv$ and $\oracleRmseLipo$
(Figure~\ref{fig:ceiling}); it is an in-sample bound for a dataset,
not a population quantity, and it is loose where most molecules have a
unique graph (Lipophilicity: $28\%$ shared). The out-of-sample
counterpart, which predicts each molecule from the other members of
its class and by the global mean when it has none, reaches only RMSE
$\looRmseEsol$, $\looRmseFsv$ and $\looRmseLipo$. Most of the
information in these benchmarks is \emph{not} carried by graph
identity alone. The oracle still settles the FreeSolv question. The
best topology-only model (Table~\ref{tab:bench}) explains far less
than the oracle allows, and the chemistry-aware reference below
explains more out of sample ($R^2\approx\rdkitRsqFsv$, computed as
$1-\mathrm{RMSE}^2/\mathrm{Var}(y)$ from Table~\ref{tab:bench}) than
any topology-only model could in sample ($\oracleRsqFsv$). The same
holds on ESOL ($\rdkitRsqEsol$ versus $\oracleRsqEsol$). The
comparison is fair in the direction that matters: with folds grouped
by graph, the cross-validated predictions of any topology-only model
are constant within an isomorphism class, so its cross-validated error
is at least the in-sample oracle's. On Lipophilicity and BBBP the
oracle is not binding ($\oracleRsqLipo$, and near one for BBBP), so
there the gap to chemistry-aware descriptors is a property of the
indices, not of the representation. On ESOL and FreeSolv the ceiling
bounds the gap between the best topological model and the oracle; it
does not by itself explain it.

\paragraph{Benchmark.} To place the indices against this ceiling we
ran a downstream benchmark on the curated data (Table~\ref{tab:bench}).
Folds are grouped by graph so that identical descriptor vectors never
appear on both sides of a split. The design is $5\times5$ repeated
cross-validation, with standardisation, screening and the choice of
the LASSO penalty all inside the training folds. Paired differences
are tested with the Nadeau--Bengio variance
correction~\cite{NadeauBengio2003}, and equivalence is assessed by two
one-sided tests (TOST)~\cite{Schuirmann1987TOST} against a margin of
$5\%$ of the reference RMSE, or $0.05\,(\mathrm{AUC}_{\mathrm{ref}}-0.5)$
for BBBP; the two one-sided tests at level $0.05$ correspond to the
reported $90\%$ intervals. As a chemistry-aware reference we used a
random forest on RDKit two-dimensional descriptors ($180$ of RDKit's
$208$).\footnote{The remaining $28$ (EState- and VSA-EState-type
descriptors, BalabanJ, BertzCT and Ipc) terminate the process under
the RDKit~2022.09 / NumPy~2 combination used here and were excluded.
Three of them are topological; their absence can only weaken the
reference, which still leads by $0.37$--$2.18$ RMSE.}

The chemistry-aware reference is far ahead of every topology-only
model: by $0.81$ RMSE on ESOL, $2.18$ on FreeSolv, $0.37$ on
Lipophilicity and $0.10$ AUC on BBBP. On ESOL and FreeSolv it exceeds,
out of sample, the in-sample graph oracle of the topology-only
representation. A second result concerns the counts. In a random
forest the ten counts $m_{ij}$ alone are not distinguishable from the
full $30$-index baseline on ESOL, FreeSolv and BBBP (intervals include
zero, although on FreeSolv the interval is wide). Only on
Lipophilicity do the distance and spectral indices add measurably
($+0.064$ RMSE for the counts alone). Restricting the baseline to its
pairwise-screened subset, and the combined screen, are discussed in
Supplementary Section~S4.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{Practical guidance.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Proposing a new degree-based index:} report its weight
vector $(f(i,j))_{(i,j)\in P_4}$ and its coefficients on a classical
basis (\texttt{certify}), and compare its single-index $R^2$ with
ordinary least squares on the ten counts.
\item \emph{Modelling with topological descriptors:} in a linear model
the ten counts $m_{ij}$ carry everything the BID indices carry, so use
the counts. In tree ensembles the benchmark shows the counts alone can
lag the full index set on some targets (Lipophilicity), so keep the
distance and spectral indices there. Expect topology-only descriptors
to add little to a chemistry-aware descriptor set (Table~\ref{tab:chem}).
\item \emph{When the result does not apply:} indices outside the BID
class (Remark~\ref{rem:scope}), datasets with atoms of degree above four
(Section~\ref{sec:general}), and non-linear models, for which only the
empirical screen of Section~\ref{sec:nonbid} speaks.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{A concrete benchmark for BID indices.} Because every BID
index lies in $\col[\mathbf 1,M]$, its in-sample $R^2$ in a
single-index linear model can never exceed the $R^2$ of ordinary least
squares on the ten counts $m_{ij}$. On our data that bound is $0.42$
(ESOL), $0.19$ (FreeSolv), $0.12$ (Lipophilicity) and $0.26$ (BBBP, as
a linear probability model), while the best of the $18$ baseline
indices, the \nCensusInst{} published instances and the $100$ Loyola points
reaches $0.36$, $0.13$, $0.05$ and $0.16$
(\texttt{results/bid\_r2\_bound.csv}). This gives a proposer of a new
BID index something concrete to report on any dataset: how close the
index comes to the bound. A modeller who wants the degree information
should simply use the ten counts.

\paragraph{What a new degree-based index must show.} On molecular
graphs (maximum degree four) a new BID index cannot be linearly
independent of a baseline that includes ten classical degree-based
indices whose weight matrix has rank ten; this is a theorem, not an
empirical finding. The
consequence is worth stating plainly. A correlation table between a
new Sombor-type variant and its predecessors, computed on a set of
octane isomers, cannot show that the variant carries anything its
predecessors do not, because on those graphs the certificate settles
that question before the table is computed. Such a table can still
bear on parsimony, on how well one number tracks a property, and that
is a legitimate but different claim. The justification for a new BID
index has to come from parsimony and interpretability (one number that
summarises the ten counts for a given property), from mathematical
properties, or from behaviour under models that are not linear in the
descriptors. Every proposal should report the weight vector
$(f(i,j))_{(i,j)\in P_4}$, which places the index exactly in the
common ten-dimensional space and makes coincidences between
differently named indices visible at once. This asks more of a
proposal than the current convention does. We think the field is
better served by it.

\paragraph{What the target-aware screen adds.} For indices outside
the BID class the question is empirical, and the answer depends on the
baseline and on the uncertainty. Against RDKit descriptors alone,
eight candidate--dataset pairs pass; against RDKit descriptors
together with the classical indices, none does
(Table~\ref{tab:chem}). Against the
topological baseline, the point-estimate ``passers'' of an earlier
version of this analysis do not survive on ESOL or FreeSolv once
intervals are reported, and most linear signals do not survive
non-linear residualization. A pairwise threshold ignores both the
multivariate baseline and the target; it is not a novelty criterion.

\paragraph{What topology cannot do.} A large share of molecules in
these benchmarks are indistinguishable to every hydrogen-suppressed,
unlabelled-graph descriptor (Section~\ref{sec:ceiling}). This is not a
defect of any particular index. It bounds the whole class, and on
FreeSolv it caps every topology-only model below what chemistry-aware
descriptors reach out of sample; the shortfall of the indices below
that cap is a separate matter.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
% =====================================================================

\begin{itemize}
\item The certificate concerns linear redundancy; it does not rank
BID indices by usefulness, and it is silent on non-linear models
except through Section~\ref{sec:nonbid}.
\item The target-aware analyses use four MoleculeNet benchmarks. The
certificate itself was checked on thirteen; datasets with hypervalent
or metal centres (HIV) leave $\Gc_4$ and require either a larger
baseline or the counts $m_{ij}$ themselves, which the data-level rank
check of Proposition~\ref{prop:cert} detects.
\item The graph oracle is an in-sample bound for a given dataset; the
RDKit reference omits $28$ descriptors for software reasons
(footnote in Section~\ref{sec:ceiling}).
\item The effect-size margin $\tau=0.10$ is a convention; we report
intervals so that readers can apply their own.
\item The non-linear check relies on random-forest residualization,
whose null widths for columns carrying no information beyond the
baseline reach $0.17$ (entropy-type functions of the certified counts).
Effects below that width are not resolved. With \nullKB{} (linear) or
\nullKC{} (non-linear) draws per dataset the smallest attainable
$p$-values are \nullPmin{} and $0.005$; the smallest $q$ reached after
correction over $80$ pairs is \qFourBbbp. The check bounds effects and
exposes artefacts; it cannot establish a non-linear signal.
\item BBBP partial correlations use a linear probability model.
\item The candidate set of Section~\ref{sec:nonbid} is illustrative,
not exhaustive.
\end{itemize}

% =====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% =====================================================================

For bond-incident-degree indices on molecular graphs, redundancy
turned out to be a matter of linear algebra rather than of correlation
thresholds. Once a baseline of classical indices spanned the edge-type
counts, every such index, published or yet to be proposed, was an
exact linear combination of the baseline and could add no linear
information about any target. The certificate held on \nGenCertified{}
of \nGenDatasets{} MoleculeNet datasets and on every published index we
tested; the one failure was diagnosed, and the spurious partial
correlations of an earlier draft were traced to an absolute tolerance.
Outside the class, no candidate passed against RDKit descriptors
combined with the classical indices, and the one non-linear signal
that survived our nulls did not pass against RDKit descriptors
alone. Graph collisions bounded
every topology-only descriptor on two benchmarks below what
chemistry-aware descriptors reached out of sample. The practical
advice is short. Use the ten counts and report the weight vector.
Correlation tables are not evidence of novelty.
